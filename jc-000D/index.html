    Let 𝑓:𝐴→𝐵 be a morphism and 𝑢:𝑈↪︎𝐵 a subobject of 𝐵. A subobject 𝑣:𝑉↪︎𝐴 of 𝐴 is called a preimage of 𝑢 under 𝑓 if for every generalized element 𝑥 of 𝐴, 𝑥∈𝑣 iff 𝑓𝑥∈𝑢.

    One can check that the preimage of 𝑢 under 𝑓 is unique (up to the equivalence ∼ of subobjects) and well defined (it does not depend on the representative of the equivalence class of subobjects):

    • If 𝑣,𝑣′ are preimages of 𝑢 under 𝑓, then 𝑣∼𝑣′, since 𝑥∈𝑣 iff 𝑓𝑥∈𝑢 iff 𝑥∈𝑣′ for every generalized element 𝑥 of 𝐴.

      Definition. monomorphism [jc-000X]

      • November 3, 2026
      • Justin

      A morphism 𝑚:𝐴→𝐵 in a category 𝒞︀ is a monomorphism (or is monic), written 𝑚:𝐴↪︎𝐵, if it can be cancelled on the left: for all morphisms 𝑥,𝑦:Γ→𝐴 with the same source,

      𝑚𝑥=𝑚𝑦⟹𝑥=𝑦.

      In the notation of generalized elements, postcomposition with 𝑚 never identifies two different generalized elements of 𝐴: for every context Γ, the function

      (𝑚∘−):hom𝒞︀(Γ,𝐴)→hom𝒞︀(Γ,𝐵)

      is injective. In Set, the monomorphisms are exactly the injective functions.

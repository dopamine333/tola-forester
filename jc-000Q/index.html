    Definition [jc-000Q]

    The subobject classifier Ω is the representing object of the subobject functor Sub. Explicitly, Ω is an object of 𝒞︀ together with a natural isomorphism

    𝜒:Sub≅hom𝒞︀(−,Ω)

    From the discussion above, we expect the naturality of 𝜒 to give us a uniform way to convert a subobject of 𝐴 into a morphism into Ω. We may also use the Yoneda lemma to find the essential core of 𝜒. Moreover, the isomorphism part of 𝜒 says that every subobject is completely characterized by an arrow into Ω. We encourage the reader to play around with the naturality of 𝜒 and see what the examples in the previous sections correspond to; you may be able to guess all the constructions we will do in the sections below.

    Let’s unpack the definition of the subobject classifier:

    Comparing these three proofs is very interesting. Personally, I like the second method the most: the Yoneda lemma gives us a strong, flexible and intuitive way to think about an object defined as the representing object of some functor. This covers examples like limits, exponential objects, the subobject classifier, and more. But in principle it can be wordy: one needs to justify that every step in the computation is natural and an isomorphism, write down how elements are sent, and check the properties.
